# Marshall Wace Track — "New Worlds"

## The brief (verbatim from the slide)

> **Make a project that explores the idea of New Worlds.**
> Build something that opens a door to the unexplored, whether that's new data, ideas or
> something novel no one has seen before.
>
> **Interpret the theme however you think best captures the spirit of this idea!**
>
> Please come and chat to us if you're looking for inspiration!

- **Prize (from the slide):** iPad + Apple Pencil, plus MW merch (T-shirt, fleece, gilet, North
  Face duffel). Unconfirmed: per member or one per team. The other three sponsors all say
  "each member".
- **Judging criteria (from the second slide):**
  > We'd like to see:
  > - **Creativity**
  > - **Technical complexity**
  > - **Good presentation**
  >
  > **Do what you're interested in!**

  No weighting was given. Ask at the stand.
- **Sat 14:00, OC0.01: a workshop, not a talk.** It's a "System Design Workshop: Data Ingestion
  Platform", with its own **prizes to win**. Go, as the whole team if possible. It shows what MW
  engineers care about (ingesting messy data at scale), and the judges will be there. The slide
  also says to come and chat for inspiration, so pitch our one-liner to them afterwards.
- **Judging:** a strict **3-minute** slot with MW. Live code on a laptop, talked through.

## Who Marshall Wace are
- London-based alternatives manager, founded 1997 by Paul Marshall and Ian Wace. ~$85bn AUM.
- Long/short equity across **quantitative/systematic and fundamental** strategies.
- Positioning: tech- and data-first for 20+ years. They build proprietary systems on
  "best-of-breed open source" with one integrated platform across the business.
- Values they use: integrity, client-first, **cognitive diversity**, "every day, our people are
  empowered to do things differently."
- TOPS framing: Talent, Operations, Products, Stewardship. Stewardship covers UN PRI signatory
  status and ESG engagement.
- From their slides: **$92.6bn AUM**, 782+ people, **~200 developers**. Flat, meritocratic: "as
  much responsibility and autonomy as you can handle".
- Roles: 10-week internship (2027/2028 grads), 6-month AI placement (London), and a rotational
  graduate scheme (London, NY, Hong Kong, Singapore).
- **What they look for:** a strong communicator, passion for technology, a STEM degree,
  critical thinking. **What they don't:** knowledge of finance, or a computer science degree.
  So the judges value clear communication and real curiosity over finance polish.

## How their briefs have changed (full text where published)
| Event | Theme | Their example directions | Judging criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| DurHack 2024 | Chain of events | "Make a game where your actions have consequences · Visualise global supply chains · Create a high-throughput data processing pipeline" | Innovation/Idea, Creativity, Learning |
| DurHack 2025 | Bridging Worlds | "Map data between domains to uncover new insights · Interface between systems that operate independently · Design a system for human-AI collaboration" | Creativity/Innovation, Technical Competence/Execution, Research/Learning, Polish/Completion |
| IC Hack 26 | Emergent Behaviour | Not published (Devpost lists only the prize name) | Not published |
| **WHACK 26** | **New Worlds** | **None.** "Opens a door to the unexplored, whether that's new data, ideas or **something novel no one has seen before**" | **Creativity, Technical complexity, Good presentation** · "Do what you're interested in!" |

**What changed this time:**
1. **Novelty is now in the brief itself.** Earlier briefs asked you to *explore an idea*. This one
   asks for something *no one has seen before*. It's a higher bar.
2. **No example directions.** In earlier years the examples steered entries into the same few
   answers. Leaving them out reads as "don't hand us a template".
3. **Creativity comes first, and "Learning" is gone.** Earlier criteria rewarded learning
   effort. Now it's creativity + technical complexity + presentation: be original, be hard, sell it.

**Steering test for any idea:** if a judge could say "I've seen that before" (a digital twin, a
traffic sim, a chatbot over X, a dashboard of Y), the novelty has to come from something else.
That might be a new framing, a new dataset, or something new you can do in it.

## What a winning entry looks like (mapped to their 3 criteria)
1. **Creativity.** "Interpret however you think best" invites a clever reading. The literal
   reading (a space game, a planet generator) will be crowded. Aim for a "door" moment: in about
   10 seconds the judge sees something they had never seen before.
2. **Technical complexity.** The judges are engineers. Show a real pipeline: genuine or
   under-explored data, or live generation, not a static mockup. Be ready to give a
   30-second "how it works" covering data, model and architecture.
3. **Good presentation.** A tight demo script, a working live happy path, and a polished first
   screen. Rehearse it before 11:00 Sunday. A robust core beats breadth.
4. **"Do what you're interested in."** Pick an angle the team actually cares about. That
   enthusiasm comes through in the pitch.

## Interpretation angles (seeds, not decisions)
- **New data:** a dataset nobody looks at turned into an explorable world, e.g. public
  filings, satellite or sensor feeds, open government data, Companies House, planning
  applications, ship/flight tracking, or Wikipedia edit streams.
- **New worlds as hidden structure:** map an invisible landscape such as the latent space of
  embeddings, market correlation "continents", or the citation graph of ideas, and let users fly
  through it.
- **Generated worlds:** a procedural or AI-generated environment grounded in real data, e.g.
  turn a company's financials or a city's data into a world you walk through.
- **New worlds of perspective:** see a place, a market or a piece of text the way someone else
  would. This ties to their "cognitive diversity" value.
- **Unexplored by people:** deep sea, space, microscopic, or very old archives (historical
  newspapers, digitised manuscripts) made navigable.
